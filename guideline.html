BEFORE A WILDFIRE

Be Prepared. Stay Protected.

Wildfires can spread quickly and cause serious damage to people, homes, wildlife, and the environment. Preparing before a wildfire occurs can help protect your family and community.

Why Is Preparation Important?

Prevention and preparedness can save lives and reduce wildfire damage.

Preparing for wildfires before they happen is one of the most effective ways to reduce risks and protect communities. By understanding local wildfire dangers, creating an evacuation plan, preparing emergency supplies, and maintaining the surroundings of our homes, we can become more resilient. Everyone has a role in reducing wildfire risks and keeping the environment safe.

6 Essential Preparation Guidelines

Follow these important steps to prepare for wildfire emergencies.

1. Understand Your Wildfire Risk

Learn whether your community is located near forests, grasslands, or dry vegetation that could catch fire easily.

  • Identify nearby wildfire-prone areas.
  • Monitor local weather and fire warnings.
  • Know the safest routes out of your area.

2. Protect Your Home

Reduce flammable materials around your house to make it less vulnerable to approaching wildfires.

  • Remove dry leaves and dead plants.
  • Keep grass short and trees maintained.
  • Store firewood away from buildings.
  • Clean roofs and gutters regularly.

3. Prepare an Emergency Kit

Prepare essential supplies in advance so your family can respond quickly during an emergency.

  • Drinking water and non-perishable food.
  • First-aid supplies and medications.
  • Flashlights and extra batteries.
  • Face masks and hygiene products.

4. Create an Evacuation Plan

Plan how your family will leave safely if a wildfire threatens your community.

  • Choose several evacuation routes.
  • Identify nearby safe meeting points.
  • Agree on an emergency contact person.
  • Plan transportation for family members.

5. Stay Informed

Keep updated with reliable information so you can make quick and safe decisions.

  • Follow official weather forecasts.
  • Listen to emergency announcements.
  • Keep your phone charged.
  • Know local emergency contacts.

6. Prepare Your Community

Working together helps communities become safer and better prepared for wildfire emergencies.

  • Share emergency contact information.
  • Identify vulnerable neighbours who may need help.
  • Keep community evacuation routes clear.
  • Participate in preparedness activities.

Build Your Emergency Kit

An emergency kit contains essential items that help you and your family stay safe during a wildfire evacuation. Keep it somewhere accessible and ready to take with you.

  • Drinking water and packaged food
  • First-aid kit and necessary medicines
  • Flashlight and extra batteries
  • Face masks and hygiene supplies
  • Important documents in waterproof bags
  • Mobile phone, charger, and power bank
  • Extra clothes and sturdy shoes
  • Essential supplies for children, older adults, and pets

Create Your Family Evacuation Plan

Make sure everyone knows what to do before a wildfire occurs.

1

Identify Safe Routes

Find at least two possible evacuation routes from your home and identify safe destinations outside wildfire-prone areas.

2

Choose a Meeting Point

Select a safe location where family members can meet if they become separated during an emergency.

3

Prepare Important Documents

Keep identification cards, medical information, emergency contacts, and important documents together in a waterproof bag.

4

Practice Your Plan

Discuss the evacuation plan with your family and practise how to leave quickly and safely.

5

Plan for Everyone

Make arrangements for children, older adults, people with disabilities, and pets who may need additional assistance.

Remember: Prepare Before It's Too Late!

Never wait until a wildfire is nearby to prepare. If authorities issue an evacuation order, leave immediately using a safe route. Do not delay evacuation to collect belongings. Your life and safety are always more important than property.
