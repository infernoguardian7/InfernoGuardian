ABOUT WILDFIRES

Understanding Wildfire Risks

Wildfires are uncontrolled fires that spread through forests, grasslands, and other areas containing vegetation. They can become more likely during hot, dry seasons when plants and surrounding materials lose moisture. Understanding how wildfires start and spread is an important first step toward preventing them and reducing their impacts.

BE READY BEFORE AN EMERGENCY

6 Important Ways to Prepare for Wildfires

Simple actions and good preparation can make a difference before a wildfire occurs.

01 / PREPARATION

Prepare Your Surroundings

Keep areas around buildings clear of unnecessary dry vegetation and easily ignited materials.

02 / EMERGENCY KIT

Prepare an Emergency Kit

Prepare essential supplies such as drinking water, food, a flashlight, batteries, and first-aid materials.

03 / FAMILY SAFETY

Create a Family Plan

Discuss emergency contacts, meeting places, and evacuation arrangements with family members.

04 / WEATHER

Monitor Weather Conditions

Pay attention to dry weather, strong winds, and official wildfire warnings in your area.

05 / PREVENTION

Use Fire Responsibly

Follow local fire-safety rules and avoid unsafe burning practices, especially during dry conditions.

06 / AWARENESS

Share Reliable Information

Help others understand wildfire risks by sharing trustworthy safety information and prevention advice.

UNDERSTAND THE CAUSES

Major Wildfire Risk Factors

Human activities and environmental conditions can contribute to the start and spread of wildfires.

Human Activities

Uncontrolled burning, unattended campfires, and discarded smoking materials can start fires.

Dry Vegetation

Dry grass, leaves, branches, and other vegetation can provide fuel for spreading fires.

Weather Conditions

High temperatures, low humidity, and strong winds can increase wildfire risk.

Natural Causes

Natural events such as lightning strikes can ignite vegetation and cause wildfires.

WHEN IS THE RISK HIGHER?

Understanding Myanmar's Dry Season

Forest fires are more common during the dry season, when vegetation becomes dry and can catch fire more easily. In Myanmar, this season generally lasts from December to May.

01 / EARLY DETECTION

Recognizing Early Warning Signs

Recognizing possible wildfire warning signs can help people respond more quickly. Smoke, unusual burning smells, visible flames, and official emergency alerts should never be ignored. Stay informed through reliable local authorities and follow their instructions.

02 / HOUSEHOLD READINESS

Create a Clear Evacuation Strategy

Quick decisions save lives when a wildfire approaches. Set up two safe meeting points, map out alternate escape roads, keep an emergency go-bag packed, and register for local alert notifications so everyone knows how to respond right away.

03 / COMMUNITY OUTREACH

Stop Wildfires Before They Start

Strong communities reduce fire risks by sharing practical safety knowledge. From maintaining defensible space around homes to practicing safe outdoor burning, local groups and schools can empower neighbors to protect nearby forests and wildlife.
